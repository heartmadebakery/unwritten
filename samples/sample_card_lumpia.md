# Lola Cely's Lumpia — the card

> On the worn kawali: "That is not dirty, that is the kawali's life. Sixty years of oil. You wash it gentle, never scrub it shiny. My apo wants to buy me a nonstick — no. This one knows already how the lumpia likes it."

1. Peel one wrapper, lay it like a diamond, keep the rest under the damp towel or they dry and crack.
2. Filling near the corner closest to you — a line, like a pencil, not too fat.
3. Fold the corner over, fold the two sides in, then roll away from you, tight, like tucking in a baby.
4. Dab water on the last corner, press, it seals.
5. Fry in the hot oil until golden.

## What Lola Cely knows

- You dip a corner of the wrapper. It should sizzle right away, no waiting.
- If it just sits there, the oil is shy, and the lumpia drinks the oil instead of frying.
- Listen — at the end they sing less. When the singing goes quiet and they're golden, they're done.
- Too much filling, they burst in the oil, everybody's filling is floating.
- Too loose, same.
- Don't crowd the kawali — they visit each other and stick together, and then you tear them pulling apart.
- Now I freeze them first, before frying — my daughter taught me that one, from the internet. More crispy. Before, straight to the oil. Still good, but frozen is more crispy. You can learn from the young ones too.
- Learned from: My mother, in Batangas. She rolled for every fiesta, hundreds. Her hands were so fast. I was the one who always tore the wrappers, she would give me the torn ones to eat with sugar. That was my job at first — eating the mistakes.
