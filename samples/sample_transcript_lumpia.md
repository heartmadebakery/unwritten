**Interviewer:** Your frying pan is completely black on the inside — tell me about it.

**Lola Cely:** That is not dirty, that is the kawali's life. Sixty years of oil. You wash it gentle, never scrub it shiny. My apo wants to buy me a nonstick — no. This one knows already how the lumpia likes it.

**Interviewer:** Now the quick steps, like jotting them on an index card. How do you roll lumpia?

**Lola Cely:** Okay. Peel one wrapper, lay it like a diamond, keep the rest under the damp towel or they dry and crack. Filling near the corner closest to you — a line, like a pencil, not too fat. Fold the corner over, fold the two sides in, then roll away from you, tight, like tucking in a baby. Dab water on the last corner, press, it seals. Then fry in the hot oil until golden.

**Interviewer:** How do you know when the oil is right?

**Lola Cely:** You dip a corner of the wrapper. It should sizzle right away, no waiting. If it just sits there, the oil is shy, and the lumpia drinks the oil instead of frying. And listen — at the end they sing less. When the singing goes quiet and they're golden, they're done.

**Interviewer:** What goes wrong if you do it the other way?

**Lola Cely:** Too much filling, they burst in the oil, everybody's filling is floating. Too loose, same. And don't crowd the kawali — they visit each other and stick together, and then you tear them pulling apart.

**Interviewer:** What do you do differently now than when you started?

**Lola Cely:** Now I freeze them first, before frying — my daughter taught me that one, from the internet. More crispy. Before, straight to the oil. Still good, but frozen is more crispy. You can learn from the young ones too.

**Interviewer:** Who taught you this?

**Lola Cely:** My mother, in Batangas. They rolled for every fiesta, hundreds. Their hands were so fast. I was the one who always tore the wrappers, they would give me the torn ones to eat with sugar. That was my job at first — eating the mistakes.
