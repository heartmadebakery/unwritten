**Interviewer:** The markings on your measuring cup are completely worn off — tell me about it.

**Nadia:** Ha! Forty years of thumbs will do that. I don't need the lines. A cup is a cup — I know where the sugar sits, I can feel the weight of it. My daughter bought me a new one with red letters. It's in the back of the drawer.

**Interviewer:** Now the quick steps, like jotting them on an index card. How do you make the tart?

**Nadia:** Shell first. Press the crust in, prick it, bake it empty with the beans on top so it doesn't puff up. While that's going, melt the butter with the sugar and syrup until it threads off the spoon. Off the heat. Let it cool a little, then the eggs, then the pecans. Pour it in the shell. Bake until the middle still wobbles a bit, like jelly that's almost set.

**Interviewer:** How do you know when it's right?

**Nadia:** The wobble. The edges are firm and the middle moves just a little when you shake the pan gentle. If it doesn't move at all, you went too far — it turns to candy and it'll break your teeth. And the syrup — if it doesn't thread, it's sugar water, wait longer.

**Interviewer:** What goes wrong if you do it the other way?

**Nadia:** Eggs in the hot syrup, you'll scramble them right in the pot. Everybody does that once. And don't skip the beans in the shell, it puffs like a pillow and there's no room for the filling.

**Interviewer:** What do you do differently now than when you started?

**Nadia:** I toast the pecans first, in a dry pan, just until you smell them. I didn't used to. Forty years to learn to toast the nuts. It tastes like more.

**Interviewer:** Who taught you this?

**Nadia:** My Aunt Rose. She sold these at the church bazaar, two dollars a tart, and there was a line. She never wrote anything down either. I watched her three Christmases before she let me pour.
