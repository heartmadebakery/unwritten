# Nadia's pecan tart — the write-up

> On the worn measuring cup: "Ha! Forty years of thumbs will do that. I don't need the lines. A cup is a cup — I know where the sugar sits, I can feel the weight of it. My daughter bought me a new one with red letters. It's in the back of the drawer."

1. Press the crust in, prick it, bake it empty with the beans on top so it doesn't puff up.
2. Melt the butter with the sugar and syrup until it threads off the spoon.
3. Off the heat. Let it cool a little, then the eggs, then the pecans.
4. Pour it in the shell. Bake until the middle still wobbles a bit, like jelly that's almost set.

## What Nadia knows

- The edges are firm and the middle moves just a little when you shake the pan gentle.
- If it doesn't move at all, you went too far — it turns to candy and it'll break your teeth.
- If it doesn't thread, it's sugar water, wait longer.
- Eggs in the hot syrup, you'll scramble them right in the pot. Everybody does that once.
- Don't skip the beans in the shell, it puffs like a pillow and there's no room for the filling.
- I toast the pecans first, in a dry pan, just until you smell them. I didn't used to. Forty years to learn to toast the nuts. It tastes like more.
- Learned from: My Aunt Rose. She sold these at the church bazaar, two dollars a tart, and there was a line. She never wrote anything down either. I watched her three Christmases before she let me pour.
